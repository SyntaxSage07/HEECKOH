5 SPSS Mistakes Every Student Makes

Jan 15, 2026 · 4 min read

SPSS is one of the most widely used tools for statistical analysis in academic research, but it's also one of the easiest places to make mistakes that quietly undermine your entire study. Here are five of the most common errors we see students make — and how to avoid them.

1. Not Cleaning Data Before Analysis

Running tests on raw, uncleaned data is one of the fastest ways to get misleading results. Missing values, duplicate entries, and inconsistent coding (like mixing "Male"/"M" in the same column) can silently skew your findings.

2. Choosing the Wrong Statistical Test

Not every research question fits a t-test or ANOVA. Using the wrong test for your data type (categorical vs. continuous) or study design often produces results that look valid but don't actually answer your research question.

3. Ignoring Assumptions

Most statistical tests have underlying assumptions — normality, homogeneity of variance, and so on. Skipping these checks can invalidate your results even if the test itself "ran successfully."

4. Misreporting p-values

A p-value under 0.05 doesn't automatically mean your finding is important or large — just that it's statistically unlikely to be random chance. Confusing statistical significance with practical significance is a very common write-up mistake.

5. Not Backing Up Your Work

A surprising number of students lose hours of analysis to a crashed file with no backup. Save versions as you go, and keep a copy of your raw data separate from your working file.
